# Readings

Everything assigned in this course is free to read online. Follow the links below. 

Note that a few links point at **accepted manuscripts** rather than the published article. Those are double-spaced and paginated differently, so page numbers will not match the version of record. Where the syllabus assigns a page range, it means the manuscript's pages. You should be able to access the published versions with your Cornell accounts. These are marked below.

## Further reading

Not required, but you can consult these if you would like:

**On the framing of the course**

- Breiman, L. 2001. "Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures." *Statistical Science* 16(3): 199–231. doi:[10.1214/ss/1009213726](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1009213726) — the origin of the statistics-versus-algorithms division, and worth arguing with rather than accepting.

**On the lecture 1 example**

- Fryer, R. G. 2019. "An Empirical Analysis of Racial Differences in Police Use of Force." *Journal of Political Economy* 127(3): 1210–1261. Response at *JPE* 128(10).
- Knox, D., W. Lowe & J. Mummolo. 2020. "Administrative Records Mask Racially Biased Policing." *American Political Science Review* 114(3): 619–637.

**Validation and resampling**

- Neunhoeffer, M. & S. Sternberg. 2019. "How Cross-Validation Can Go Wrong and What to Do About It." *Political Analysis* 27(1): 101–106. doi:[10.1017/pan.2018.39](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2018.39)
- GCR ch. 12 — spatial cross-validation. Random k-fold on spatial data is wrong.

**Interpretability**

- Molnar, C. *Interpretable Machine Learning*, 3rd ed. <https://christophm.github.io/interpretable-ml-book/> — the reference for partial dependence, permutation importance and SHAP.
- Funk, K. D., H. L. Paul & A. Q. Philips. 2022. "Point Break: Using Machine Learning to Uncover a Critical Mass in Women's Representation." *Political Science Research and Methods* 10(2): 372–390. **CC BY 4.0.** A published application of the tools the Oct 15 reading objects to.

**Hierarchical models and MRP**

- Hanretty, C. 2020. "An Introduction to Multilevel Regression and Post-Stratification for Estimating Constituency Opinion." *Political Studies Review* 18(4): 630–645. The procedure written down, which the assigned reading does not do. Free manuscript: <https://chrishanretty.co.uk/pubs/10.1177_1478929919864773/fulltext.pdf>
- Warshaw, C. & J. Rodden. 2012. "How Should We Measure District-Level Public Opinion on Individual Issues?" *Journal of Politics* 74(1): 203–219.

**Text and topic models**

- Blei, D. M. 2012. "Probabilistic topic models." *Communications of the ACM* 55(4): 77–84. doi:[10.1145/2133806.2133826](https://doi.org/10.1145/2133806.2133826) Eight pages, and the clearest short statement of what a topic model is.

**Audio**

- Dietrich, B. J., R. D. Enos & M. Sen. 2019. "Emotional Arousal Predicts Voting on the U.S. Supreme Court." *Political Analysis* 27(2): 237–243. doi:[10.1017/pan.2018.47](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2018.47)
